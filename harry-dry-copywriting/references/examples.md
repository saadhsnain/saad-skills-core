# Copywriting Examples

Detailed breakdowns of great copy and why it works.

## Apple iPod

**Line:** "1,000 songs in your pocket"

**Why it works:**
- Visualizable: Picture your pocket full of songs
- Metonymy: "Pocket" stands in for "media player"—more visual, more surprising
- Concrete vs abstract: PC ads talked about "4GB storage." Apple talked about experience
- Result: Everyone had an iPod. Everyone walked around with white earbuds.

## Hinge

**Line:** "The dating app designed to be deleted"

**Why it works:**
- Juxtaposition: Who makes an app that wants to be deleted?
- Alliteration: D-D-D (dating, designed, deleted)
- Unique: No competitor can own this positioning now
- Implies the promise: You'll meet someone and live happily ever after

**Process:** Brief was "an app for people who want long-term relationships, fed up of dating apps." Writer turned that into something memorable.

## The Economist

**Line:** "I've never read The Economist" — Management trainee, aged 42

**Why it works:**
- Visual hierarchy: Read big text first, punchline hits second
- Doesn't look like an ad: No logo, no picture (Neil French: "If you can do an ad with just one element, you're onto a winner")
- Tells a story: Implication is clear without being preachy
- Economist red: The color itself is branding

## Corvette

**Line:** "They don't write songs about Volvos"

**Why it works:**
- Enemy: Taking a shot at Volvo
- Unique: Post Malone sings about Corvettes. Country singers love Corvettes. It's romantic.
- Six words: Lots of white space
- One Mississippi, two Mississippi: You get it instantly

## Tesla (Ad Professor)

**Line:** "It takes 3.1 seconds to read this ad—the same time it takes a Model S to go from 0 to 60"

**Why it works:**
- Falsifiable: You can verify this is true
- Breaks the fourth wall: The ad talks about itself
- Precise: 3.1 seconds feels exact and researched
- First line, second line: You need to read line one to understand line two

## Athletic Greens

**Line:** "You're going to need a smaller cabinet"

**Why it works:**
- Positioning 101: What do you replace? This does everything in one go
- Visualizable: You can see the cabinet full of vitamins
- Pain point: Morning routine with vitamin A, D, C, zinc... this is all-in-one
- Risk that pays off: Not everyone gets it, but the in-crowd does—and they talk about it

## Volkswagen (Winter Prep)

**Layout:** Ordinary car: drain radiator, flush, check hose, refill... (7 steps). Volkswagen: change the oil (1 step).

**Why it works:**
- Shows simplicity through contrast
- Enemy structure: Complexity vs simplicity
- Visual layout does the work
- You don't need to read—you see it

## New Balance

**Line:** "Worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio"

**Why it works:**
- Visualizable: Supermodel in London, dad at Ohio barbecue
- Falsifiable: True—supermodels have worn them, dads in Ohio do wear them
- Unique: Prada isn't worn by dads at barbecues. Reebok isn't a supermodel favorite.
- Juxtaposition creates humor and memorability

## Debeers Diamond

**Line:** "How can you make 2 months' salary last forever? The diamond engagement ring."

**Why it works:**
- Juxtaposition: 2 months vs forever—shouldn't work together
- Creates the category: Before this, wedding rings were sapphire, ruby, emerald
- Result: 85% of engagement rings are now diamonds

## Saab

**Line:** "The sport sedan for people who inherited brains instead of wealth"

**Why it works:**
- Makes the customer feel smart
- Positioning weakness as strength: Not the coolest car, but the smart choice
- Contrast: Brains vs wealth
- Appeals to identity: Everyone wants to be the person with brains

## The Secret Footballer (Dave Kitson)

**Not an ad, but perfect positioning.**

Dave Kitson: Innocuous career, one England cap, ginger striker at Reading.

Problem: "A book by David Kitson"—no one cares.

Solution: Behind-the-scenes exposé. Player gambling addictions. What happens when England goes abroad. Sign it as "The Secret Footballer."

Result: Outsold Beckham, Lampard, Gerrard, and Owen combined.

**Lesson:** Kitson's product was objectively worse than Beckham's. But by positioning it, by a little storytelling, he outsold them all. That's copywriting.

## Harry's Course Ad (Process Breakdown)

**Final ad:**
- Want to grow twice as fast?
- You have two choices
- Throw money and pray: Raise twice the cash, hire twice the staff, spend twice as much on ads, cross both your fingers
- Learn copywriting: Increase conversions from 1% to 2% (that's twice as many)

**Process:**

1. **Seed:** Tweet from 4 years ago: "The difference between 1% and 2% is not just 1%, it's 100%"

2. **First draft:** "Want to grow twice as fast? You've got two choices. Spend twice as much on ads or increase landing page conversions from 1% to 2%."

3. **Inspiration:** Volkswagen winter prep ad—loved the layout showing simplicity vs complexity

4. **Added conflict structure:** Left side (corporate way) vs right side (learn to write)

5. **Problem:** Right side was two lines—too messy

6. **Solution:** Remove "landing page." Move "learn to write" to header.

7. **New problem:** Headers lacked parallelism. "Corporate way" vs "Learn to write" = no symmetry

8. **Brainstorm headers:** Go fool Zuckerberg, Scroll down, Roll Monopoly dice, Hidden hope, Spaghetti at war...

9. **Final header:** "Throw money and pray" (parallels "Learn copywriting")

10. **Polish:** Bold "twice" throughout for rhythm

11. **Illustrations:** Fiverr artist. Feedback: "Make it same person. Guy looks pissed on left (he's an idiot). Happy on right. Turn pen into typewriter, add light bulb."

**Time:** 2 days, 25 rewrites
